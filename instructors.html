An Art School Story

Before there was Zoom Art School, there were two individual journeys — one shaped by art and one shaped by education.

Mahesh and Maitri eventually brought those journeys together to create a space where students could learn, experiment and discover the joy of art.

Mahesh Kumar · Founder & Instructor

An artist began by sharing his work. An educator began by sharing her knowledge. Together, they built a school.

01
The Artist · The Founder

Mahesh

Founder & Instructor

Almost two decades ago, before Zoom Art School existed, Mahesh's journey began with his own artwork.

Working with oil paintings and large portraits, he developed his practice as an artist and began sharing his work through exhibitions in Bengaluru, including exhibitions at Karnataka Chitrakala Parishath.

But the exhibitions brought him something he hadn't necessarily expected.

People wanted to know how the paintings were created. They wanted to understand drawing, technique and the process behind the artwork.

And gradually, Mahesh began teaching.

What started as an artist sharing his knowledge grew into something much more meaningful: helping other people discover that they too could create.

“The journey began with creating art. It grew into helping others discover theirs.”

Teaching introduced Mahesh to another side of creativity. A finished painting may belong to the artist, but watching a student gain confidence and create something of their own brings a very different kind of satisfaction.

Over the years, his experience as an artist, exhibitor and teacher became the foundation for what would eventually become Zoom Art School.

02
The Educator · The Co-Founder

Maitri

Co-Founder & Instructor

Mrs. Maitri Kumar's professional journey is one of resilience, adaptability and an enduring passion for education and the arts.

Her journey began at Kodai International School, where she started her career as a school teacher. It was there that her foundation in teaching and mentoring young learners took shape.

As the technology industry expanded, Maitri made a bold transition into the corporate world, joining ICICI OneSource and later Accenture. She progressed from customer service into soft-skills and process training, helping people develop both professional and interpersonal skills.

But while her professional life was changing, art never disappeared from it.

Throughout her corporate career, Maitri continued conducting art classes for children on weekends. What began as a hobby gradually became something much more meaningful.

Her focus was never simply on teaching children to draw. She wanted them to observe, explore, think creatively and learn through art.

In 2018, a severe episode of lower-back pain forced her to step away from her corporate career. She subsequently joined Stonehill International School, seeking a more sustainable professional path.

Then the pandemic changed the world.

Maitri and Mahesh continued their art classes online, helping children remain connected with creativity and learning during lockdown.

“What started as a weekend hobby gradually became a dedicated art education initiative.”

As the world began returning to normal, the classes flourished. More children joined, and the Junior Art batches emerged as an important part of the school's journey.

Today, Maitri continues to nurture her Junior Art students with a vision of reaching many more young minds in the years ahead.

The Story Continues

Two journeys.
One shared
vision.

Mahesh came to teaching through art. He began with paintings, portraits and exhibitions, and discovered the joy of helping others learn what he had spent years practising himself.

Maitri came to art education through teaching. From the classroom to corporate training, and from weekend art classes to online learning, her journey brought together her love of education and creativity.

The artist and the educator found their common canvas.
